A 4,600-year pollen record of mixed-forest decline at Linden Pond, Minnesota, USA

Jordan Q. Hartley (j.hartley@example.edu) and Ana Moreno

Journal of Imaginary Paleoecology 12 (2021): 45-67

Abstract
We present a pollen record from Linden Pond, a small kettle lake in Hennepin County, Minnesota. The record documents the
transition from pine–oak woodland to spruce-dominated forest with expanding prairie herbs.

2. Study site
Linden Pond (44°58'12"N, 93°15'36"W; 287 m a.s.l.) is a 4 ha kettle pond occupying a depression in glacial outwash.
The pond has a maximum depth of 6.4 m. Surrounding vegetation is a mosaic of oak savanna and cultivated land.

3. Methods
Two overlapping cores were collected in February 2019 from the ice surface in 6.2 m water depth using a Livingstone corer.
Samples of 1 cm3 were taken every 20 cm and processed with standard acetolysis; Lycopodium tablets were added as an exotic
marker. A minimum of 300 terrestrial pollen grains was counted per level by A. Moreno.
